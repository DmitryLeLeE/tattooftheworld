The Flash Sheet

Ten traditions, 86 plates from the archive — pick a design off the wall, every one opens into its own story.

Japan Irezumi Full-body suits of koi, dragons and peonies, worked by hand with tebori needles. Aotearoa · New Zealand Tā moko Carved spirals of ancestry and rank — a face that can be read like a signature. Samoa Tatau The unbroken Polynesian line — and the word the whole world borrowed: tatau. Thailand Sak Yant Sacred geometry and Khmer script, tattooed by monks and charged with protective magic. United States American Traditional Bold will hold: anchors, swallows and roses from the sailor ports of two world wars. Philippines Batok Mountain-province geometry tapped in with a thorn — kept alive by the mambabatok of Buscalan. North Africa Amazigh Tattoos Chin, brow and hands marked with palm, diamond and dot — the oldest ink of the Maghreb. Borneo Dayak Tattoos The bunga terung rosette on the shoulders of travellers — ink as a map of journeys. Hokkaidō · Japan Ainu Sinuye The smile tattooed around women's lips — a northern tradition the empire tried to erase. Russia Vory Tattoos Stars, cupolas and epaulettes — the coded service record of the Soviet prison world.
