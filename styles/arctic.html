The Arctic

Kakiniit

ᑲᑭᓐᓃᑦ · Inuit women's tattoos

Across the top of the world, from Siberia to Greenland, Inuit women wore tattoos stitched into the skin — thread, soot and a lifetime of meaning, sewn line by line.

Arctic tattooing is ancient and, uniquely, sewn. In skin-stitching, a needle draws a sinew thread blackened with soot or lamp-black through the skin, leaving a dotted line that heals solid; hand-poking filled in the rest. The oldest known tattooed skin in the Americas — a 1,600-year-old mummy from Saint Lawrence Island — carries exactly the patterns photographed on living women there in 1900.

The marks — kakiniit; the facial ones tunniit — were women's writing. Chin stripes received at first menstruation announced adulthood; cheek and brow marks, and the dense forearm and thigh patterns, recorded marriages, children, skills, and beauty. A woman's tattoos were her credentials in this world and the next: in some traditions only properly tattooed women passed fully into the land of the dead.

A 1654 Dutch painting of four Inuit from Greenland — the earliest European portrait of Inuit people — already shows the chin lines. Then came the missionaries, for whom the marks were heathen: across the twentieth century tattooing was shamed and preached nearly out of existence, and by the 1980s only a few tattooed elders remained.

The return began with their granddaughters. Inuit filmmaker Alethea Arnaquq-Baril's documentary Tunniit (2010) and the Inuit Tattoo Revitalization Project led by Hovak Johnston brought skin-stitching back as living practice: today hundreds of Inuit women across Alaska, Canada and Greenland wear new kakiniit, sewn the old way, often by artists who learned specifically to give their communities the marks the church took.

Field notes

Where
Siberia, Alaska, Canada, Greenland
Technique
Skin-stitching with soot-blackened sinew
Oldest proof
Tattooed mummy, c. 400 AD, St Lawrence Is.
Suppressed
By missionaries, 20th century
Revived
Tunniit film (2010); revitalization projects

Classic motifs

  • chin stripes (tunniit)
  • V on the brow
  • cheek fans
