Borneo

Dayak Tattoos

Iban · Kayan · Kenyah

Among the Dayak peoples of Borneo, tattoos were a travelogue: each journey, skill and deed earned its mark, until a life could be read off the skin.

Borneo's longhouse peoples — Iban, Kayan, Kenyah, Ngaju and others — built one of the great tattoo cultures of the world. Designs were carved in relief on wooden blocks, inked with soot, stamped on the skin and then worked in with two sticks: a needle set in one, tapped with the other — the hand-tap method that modern revivalists across the world still call 'Borneo style'.

For Iban men the sequence began with the bunga terung, the eggplant flower, one rosette on each shoulder — the mark of bejalai, the journey a young man makes into the world to gain knowledge and standing. Throat pieces, dog-dragons (aso), scorpions and hornbills followed particular achievements; in the old head-taking era, hand tattoos announced the gravest deed of all. Kayan and Kenyah women bore the finest work — dense lace from knuckle to thigh, applied over years, a mark of endurance and status.

The patterns are a shared vocabulary with Dayak carving and beadwork: curling fern shoots, the aso dragon-dog that guards against spirits, hornbill heads for the upper world. Missionaries and colonial administrations discouraged the practice through the twentieth century, and by its end full traditional coverage had become rare.

Revival came from both directions: urban Dayak artists reclaiming hand-tap technique and longhouse iconography, and the global craft adopting Borneo rosettes and scrollwork — often loosely. On the island itself the bunga terung is again a common sight on young shoulders: the journey mark, for a generation whose bejalai may be a flight to Kuala Lumpur rather than a river passage.

Field notes

Peoples
Iban, Kayan, Kenyah, Ngaju
Method
Hand-tap; carved block stencils
First mark
Bunga terung — journey rosette
Women's work
Dense Kayan forearm-to-thigh lace
Meaning
Journeys, deeds, protection

Classic motifs

  • bunga terung rosette
  • aso dragon-dog
  • fern scroll
  • hornbill
